## Why these two (and what was left out)

The two options differ on **what the page is about**, which changes the order of the messages, what the reader sees first, which persona pains it can touch and which open questions block it:

| | **A · Find, fix, approve** | **B · One store, with AI on top** |
|---|---|---|
| The page is about | the AI loop (scan, proposed fix, seller approves) | the product: ShopBase 2.0 as one store platform; AI is the layer on it |
| Reader state it assumes | a seller who has not diagnosed why shoppers leave; may not know "CR" or "AOV" | a seller who already runs a store on some platform and is comparing; existing merchants |
| Where the USP sits | the whole story | beats 3 to 5 of 6 |
| Open questions it depends on | few (approval behaviour, illustrations) | many ("built in", "No apps to stitch together", Free Launch plan, what changes for merchants) |

Differences considered and **not** offered as an option:
- **"You stay in control" (trust) vs "AI finds what you cannot see" (discovery).** The control-led story needs a dedicated control block. The user decided on 2026-10-24 (Step 09 Q1) that "You approve every change" stays only as the screenshot sentence, with no control block until the behaviour, including undo, is confirmed. It would break that decision. Discovery is already the spine of A.
- **Sellers who know their conversion problem vs those who do not.** Folded into A vs B: A speaks to the undiagnosed (the scan finds it), B to sellers who judge platforms as a whole. As a stand-alone axis it would give two variants of A, because both would lead with the loop. Also, the interim VoC shows the conversion pain thinly (A6: 2 of 22 sources) and shows no one using "CR" or "AOV" (07 "Their language"), so a "you already know your CR" story has no support.

## Option A — Find, fix, approve

**One-line idea.** The whole page walks through what CR Scan AI does, as a loop the reader can see and check: it scans the store, proposes a fix, the seller approves. AOV AI is shown as the second tool of the same kind. ShopBase is the place where that happens.

### Message order (7 beats, one message each; these are not sections)

| # | Beat label | The one message |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Promise | ShopBase 2.0 has AI that finds what stops shoppers and builds the fix (hero; sign-up CTA; "Explore CR Scan AI" jumps to beat 3) |
| 2 | The unseen problem | A store can have visitors and still lose them for reasons the dashboard does not show *(framing statement only; no statistic; `ASSUMPTION`, see Assumptions 4)* |
| 3 | Scan | CR Scan AI scans the store and lists what it found *(illustration 1)* |
| 4 | Fix | It builds a proposed fix for what it found *(illustration 2; screenshot "builds the fix"; whether there is one fix per finding is not stated in any input)* |
| 5 | Approve | The seller approves the fix before it goes live: the screenshot sentence "You approve every change" sits under illustration 3 as its caption, **not** as a block *(UNPROVEN as product behaviour until Step 09 Q1 is answered)* |
| 6 | Order value | AOV AI recommends offers aimed at order value, the same shape: proposal, then approval *(softened wording, awaits the user's accept or reject; no matching VoC pain)* |
| 7 | Start | Same primary button as the hero: "Get started for free". Optional one line: ShopBase 2.0 is one product (known fact, Decisions log) |

### Hero promise and what the reader sees first

- **Hero promise (headline-level, descriptive, flagged):** "AI that finds what stops shoppers on your store and builds the fix."
  - It describes the capability from the screenshot's sub-line. The screenshot's own headline ("AI that turns visitors into buyers, and every order into a bigger one.") is an outcome promise and stays out unless the Content reviewer signs it off (`09-messaging.md` Claims 1 to 3, Gate 5).
  - Sub-line carries the screenshot sentence "You approve every change" as that sentence only. Nothing about "built in", "No apps to stitch together" or "Free Launch plan" (unconfirmed, `09-messaging.md` Claims 7 to 9).
- **First thing the reader sees:** the headline, the single primary button, and directly under it **illustration 1 (the scan listing what it found)**, drawn as one white card on a colour field. It carries a visible label "Illustration". No number inside the card (Step 10 imagery rule 2).
- **Secondary CTA "Explore CR Scan AI": supported.** Beats 3 to 5 are exactly the CR Scan AI explanation, one scroll away, so the anchor has a real target (`03-brief-analysis.md` Q5 default: on-page anchor).

### Flow per persona (arrival to CTA)

**A · Experienced dropshipper**
1. Arrives with a store and ad spend already running; reads the headline and sees what the AI does in one sentence.
2. Sees illustration 1: something concrete on a store, not an assistant chat (the opposite of the category's common "AI assistant").
3. Scrolls the loop (beats 3 to 5): found problem, proposed fix, approval.
4. Reads beat 6 (AOV AI) as the second tool; moves on.
5. Clicks "Get started for free" (hero or beat 7).
- Pains touched: A6 traffic without sales, partly *(VoC, interim)*. **Not touched:** A1 costs, A2 payment holds, A3 bans, A4 ad accounts, A5 fulfilment (all listed in `09-messaging.md` "Not claimed"). The page says nothing about them.

**B · Experienced POD seller**
1. Arrives; reads the same headline. Nothing is POD-specific.
2. Sees the scan illustration; the loop applies to any store.
3. Follows beats 3 to 5; beat 6 for order value.
4. Clicks "Get started for free".
- Honest gap: the POD pains B1 margin, B2 production, B3 defects, B4 support are **not touched** (07 "Fit with the USP"); B5 "changes without consent" is touched only as a trust condition by beat 5 *(VoC, interim)*. In A, persona B gets the same page as persona A; that is a fact of the USP, not a choice of this option.

**Existing merchant**
1. Arrives already knowing ShopBase as "build a store in a few clicks" (the old live H1; 04 audit OLD-02).
2. Sees a different promise at once: AI that works on the store. One-line "one product" at beat 7 (if kept) is the only change signal.
3. Beat 5 answers E5 "will the AI change my live store on its own?", **only if** the behaviour is confirmed.
4. Plan, price, migration, old-brand accounts, support route (E1 to E4, E6 to E8) are **not answered**; the page says nothing, per the default.
5. Uses the header login (an `ASSUMPTION`: the nav is a Step 12 decision) or the same CTA.
- This option gives merchants a new-feature story, not a "what changes for me" story.

### Best for

- **Audience state:** a seller experienced in the category but **not yet diagnosing** conversion, who can be shown a concrete loop in about three scrolls. Fits readers who have not heard of "CR" or "AOV" (07 "Their language", interim).
- **Type C:** new visitors get a complete, checkable story with no dependence on open product facts. Existing merchants get the new promise but **no answers to what changes for them**; that stays unknown (`09-messaging.md` "Change message").

### Pros (3)

1. **It answers the "me too" risk by showing the feature, not by repeating outcome words.** 4 of 5 competitor homepages already make an AI claim, and Wix puts "Uplift conversions" and "Increase your average order value" under named AI features (06 "Me too" test; 06 implications 1 and 2). A scan-propose-approve sequence in illustrations is the only difference the page can show (06 me-too points 1 and 2). Pain id: A6, partly *(VoC, interim)*.
2. **It matches the proof the user chose and the best patterns found.** The user chose a product demonstration, with no numbers (Decisions log 2026-10-24). Step 08's pattern table recommends a hero with a product view and a "scan, proposed fix, you approve" step (08 pattern table rows "Hero", "Proof of the product doing the AI task", "How it works / approval step"; references R1 to R4). Pain id: A6 *(VoC, interim)*.
3. **It needs no unconfirmed product claim, so it is the least blocked.** "Built in", "No apps to stitch together" and "Free Launch plan" are all unconfirmed (03 R2, R3; 09 Claims 7 to 9). A leaves them out entirely. The approval beat also faces the AI fear of changes made without asking (AI-1; B5 consent part), which has a direct VoC fit *(VoC, interim; the fear comes from other companies' AI, an inference)* (07 "AI that changes the store"; 09 Support 3).

### Cons / risks (3) and how the page handles each

1. **AOV AI has no matching VoC pain** (09 Support 2: "None in the VoC (gap)"; 07: "AOV" appears only once, in a vendor line). *Handling:* beat 6 is short, last in the loop and written as the same proposal-then-approval shape; no pain is invented for it; it is not used in the headline; B1 margin is never implied. The Content reviewer may cut it without breaking the story.
2. **The AI claim is category-common and unproven** (06: 4 of 5 pages; every part of the USP is `UNPROVEN`, strategy pack). If the illustrations are weak or missing, the page falls back to the same kind of claim as Wix. *Handling:* no outcome sentence stands alone; each of beats 3 to 6 is paired with an illustration; no comparison and no number; "Illustration" label on each; Step 18 uses a labelled placeholder if the user's illustrations are late (09 open Q2 default).
3. **It rests on one pain that the sources show thinly and leaves the big pains unanswered.** A6 appears in 2 of 22 sources and neither reviewer describes conversion work (07 A rank 6, interim); payment holds, bans, ad blocks, fulfilment and margin are not answered (07 "Pain points the USP does not answer"). *Handling:* the page does not pretend to cover them (no "everything you need" filler; `09-messaging.md` "Not claimed"); beat 2 is a plain framing, not a claimed pain; the gap is recorded for the user, not hidden.

### Cost

- About **5 sections** (7 beats; 5 if beats 3 to 5 share one loop section, 4 if beat 2 also merges into the hero or beat 3, up to 7 if nothing merges). Step 12 decides. `ASSUMPTION`, rough estimate.
- Page length: about **5 to 6 desktop screens at 1440 px** (`ASSUMPTION`, rough estimate, not measured; the live page's rendered height was not measured either, `04-audit.md` has HTML counts only). Shortest of the two.
- Illustrations needed: 3 for the loop plus 1 for AOV AI (user-supplied).

## Option B — One store, with AI on top

**One-line idea.** ShopBase 2.0 is one store platform, and the AI works on that store. The page first says what ShopBase is now (one product, for the AI era), then shows CR Scan AI and AOV AI as the layer on top, with the seller approving.

### Message order (6 beats)

| # | Beat label | The one message |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Promise | ShopBase for the AI era: one store platform, with AI that finds what stops shoppers and builds the fix (hero; sign-up CTA; "Explore CR Scan AI" jumps to beat 3) |
| 2 | One product | ShopBase 2.0 is one independent product for running a store. Lists only the parts the user **confirms** (store, catalog, payments, fulfillment, support: **each UNCONFIRMED**); the words "built in" and "No apps to stitch together" appear only if confirmed |
| 3 | AI layer: scan and fix | CR Scan AI finds what stops shoppers on this store and builds the fix *(illustration)* |
| 4 | AI layer: order value | AOV AI recommends offers aimed at order value *(softened wording; no matching VoC pain)* |
| 5 | You approve | The screenshot sentence "You approve every change" as the caption under the illustration of the approval step, **not** as a block *(UNPROVEN as product behaviour)* |
| 6 | Start | "Get started for free". "Free Launch plan" appears only if confirmed; otherwise the button stands alone |

### Hero promise and what the reader sees first

- **Hero promise (headline-level, descriptive, flagged):** "ShopBase 2.0: one store platform, with AI that finds what stops shoppers and builds the fix."
  - "One" rests on the user's decision that ShopBase 2.0 is one independent product (one brand instead of three). It must not be read or rewritten as "all-in-one" or as a coverage claim ("built in"); the Content reviewer checks this at Gate 5. "Store platform" is my wording (09 Assumption 2). No result, no number, no comparison.
  - The sub-line carries "You approve every change" as the screenshot sentence only.
- **First thing the reader sees:** the headline, the single primary button, and under it **one illustration of a store with the AI scan card on top of it** (the AI shown as a layer on a store, not alone). If beat 2 survives confirmation, the part names sit as small labels on that picture; otherwise the picture is the store plus the scan card only. Labelled "Illustration"; no number.
- **Secondary CTA "Explore CR Scan AI": supported, but one beat further down than in A.** Beat 3 exists and is the target; it is not the first thing after the hero, so the anchor is a jump past beat 2. Acceptable; if beat 2 grows, drop the secondary button.

### Flow per persona (arrival to CTA)

4. Clicks "Get started for free".

**B · Experienced POD seller**
1. Arrives; reads the same promise.
2. Beat 2: fulfilment and catalog are the parts that matter to a POD seller, but they are **UNCONFIRMED**, and the old hub services (PrintHub, PlusHub) are superseded or unknown (strategy pack Q7). The page names no old brand and makes no cost, margin or speed claim.

4. Clicks "Get started for free".
- Not touched: B1 margin, B2 production, B3 defects, B4 support (07 "Fit with the USP"). Beat 2 is the only place POD concerns could show, and only if confirmed.

**Existing merchant**
1. Arrives knowing ShopBase as the quick store builder with three tabs (04 audit OLD-04).
2. The headline and beat 2 tell them the product is now one product; this is the only option that has a beat for it (it partly answers E3: the old brand names are gone, with nothing about accounts or services).

4. E1, E2, E4, E6, E7, E8 are **not answered**; the page says nothing, per the default. E6 "do my apps and payments still work" is the one most likely to be triggered by beat 2, and the page cannot answer it.

### Best for

- **Audience state:** a seller who **already runs a store on some platform** and reads the page as "what is this platform now, and is it worth a look". Best for readers who compare platforms and for those who knew the old ShopBase.
- **Type C:** serves existing merchants better than A on **what ShopBase is now**, but only to the extent the user confirms the product facts. New visitors get a platform story with AI on top, which is the category's usual frame. What changes for merchants stays unknown.

### Pros (3)

1. **It gives type C readers the "what is ShopBase 2.0 now" answer.** The old page sold three products by audience and named dead brands (04 audit "What does not work" 2 and 3); the reader "must stop believing" in three products and in PrintBase and PlusBase as brands (06 "Old vs new"). (06 "Me too" test, row 2); superlative or "all-in-one" framing appears on 4 of 5 homepages (06 category patterns). *Handling:* the page does not use "all-in-one"; the ShopBase 2.0 name and the AI beats carry the difference; beats 3 to 5 get the same illustrations as A; no comparison. The risk is still higher than in A, because a platform frame sits closer to the category.
3. **AOV AI has no VoC pain, and the platform lead dilutes the AI-first decision.** (09 Support 2 gap; the user decided the USP is AI-first, Decisions log.) With the AI starting at beat 3, a skimmer may read a feature list. *Handling:* AI is in the hero headline; beat 2 is one beat only (N5); beat 4 is short; the AI beats keep the same illustrations. Step 12 can still put the AI loop ahead if the user prefers.

### Cost

## Recommendation & why

*(This is a recommendation. The user chooses; I do not decide for them, N6.)*

**I recommend Option A.**

1. **It is the only option the page can write today without an unconfirmed product-coverage or pricing claim.** B needs the user to confirm "built in", "No apps to stitch together" and "Free Launch plan" first (03 R2, R3; 09 Claims 7 to 9). A still rests on things that are not proven: every AI description is `UNPROVEN`, the approval behaviour is unconfirmed (A uses it only as the screenshot sentence, 09 Q1), and the illustrations are planned but do not exist yet (Decisions log, Step 09 Q2). B shares all of these.
2. **It answers the Step 06 "me too" finding with what the page can show.** The only differences from the five homepages are a scan-and-fix loop and an approval step, and A gives those the whole page (06 implications 2 and 3).
3. **It keeps the user's AI-first decision and one focal point per beat** (Decisions log; N5).

**When B would be the better choice:** if the user wants Home to tell existing merchants "what ShopBase is now" more than to explain the AI, **and** confirms which of store, catalog, payments, fulfillment and support really ship in 2.0, **and** confirms or drops the Free Launch plan line. In that case B is the stronger type C story.

**What A does not do, stated plainly:** it does not answer existing merchants' questions (E1 to E8), and it leaves payment holds, bans, ad blocks, fulfilment and margin unanswered (07 "Pain points the USP does not answer"). If the user wants the page to cover those, neither option does; it needs new input at Step 01 or 09.

**If A is chosen:** a single "one product" line fits at beat 7 as a known fact, with no old brand named. The user may decide at Step 12 whether to keep it.
