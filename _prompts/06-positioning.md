# Step 06 · Positioning & competitors

> **Phase 2 · Research** — role: Head of Product, Market analyst
> **Applies to:** A B C
> **Output:** `03-research/06-positioning.md`
> **Base prompt** — never edited for a project. Shared rules and the end-of-turn block: `_prompts/README.md`.

---

## Reads

- `01-strategy/01-strategy-pack.md` · `03-research/03-brief-analysis.md` · `03-research/04-audit.md` (if B/C)

## Stop if

- Gate 1 is not `APPROVED` in `_progress.md`.

## Do

1. **Direct competitors — 3 to 5.** Real products, named, each with a source link (web search). **No web access?** Say so at the top, use only well-known products, mark every fact `ASSUMPTION — not verified online`, and ask the user for the competitors they care about.

   | Competitor | Type (direct / indirect) | How they position (their own headline + promise) | USP they claim | Where they look like everyone else | Link |

2. **Category patterns** — what every competitor page does, so visitors expect it (hero + product shot, logos, pricing link…).
3. **The "me too" test** — would our promise, written plainly, be confused with any competitor's? Quote the closest competitor line and say what makes ours different, using the USP and its proof from the strategy pack. If the difference is not provable, say so.
4. **Our position** — one sentence: for whom, what we do, why it is different.
5. **Type C only:** compare the **old** positioning (from the audit) with the **new** one. What the reader must now believe, and what they must stop believing.
6. **Design and content implications** — 5–8 lines: "Because ___, the page must ___."

## Output — `03-research/06-positioning.md`

```
# 06 · Positioning & competitors

```

## Check before handing back

- [ ] At least 3 real competitors, each with a source link (or the no-web warning).
- [ ] The me-too test quotes a real competitor line.
- [ ] Our position matches the USP in the strategy pack and does not add claims.
- [ ] Every implication points back to a finding.
